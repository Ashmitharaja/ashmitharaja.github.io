Featured publication

Directional Curvature from Armijo Backtracking

A Low-Cost Sharpness Probe and a Calibration-Free Learning-Rate Safeguard for Adam

Ashmitha R, Jörg Frochte · Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR), accepted September 2026 · arXiv:2607.03998 (July 2026)

My video walkthrough of the paper · Watch on YouTube

Why The problem

Adam is the default optimizer for deep learning, but one bad learning rate can make training diverge. On large models, a divergence wastes hours of compute and is usually caught only after the loss has blown up. Existing fixes need hand-tuned schedules or expensive second-order computation.

What The idea

Run a classical Armijo backtracking line search along Adam's own preconditioned direction. It needs about five forward evaluations and no calibration. The number of backtracks doubles as an online probe of directional curvature: a cheap signal of how sharp the loss surface is right now.

Where The evidence

Evaluated on vision models and on GPT-2 pretraining up to 355M parameters. The safeguard prevented divergence in these settings with less than 1% wall-clock overhead.

Research interests

Questions I want to work on

I'm drawn to problems where a small piece of mathematics changes how large models behave in practice.

Optimization & training stability

Adaptive optimizers, curvature and sharpness, and learning-rate safeguards that don't need hand-tuning.

Why: as models grow, every failed run gets more expensive. I want instability to be detectable early, cheaply and automatically.

Reliable LLM systems

Retrieval-augmented generation and tool use through the Model Context Protocol (MCP) for high-stakes domains such as cardiology and regulatory compliance.

Why: in medicine and law, a fluent but ungrounded answer is worse than none. Answers must cite sources and be checkable.

Memory for agents

Treating memory as a mathematical object: operator-theoretic formulations, stability guarantees and selective forgetting.

Why: long-horizon agents fail when they remember everything, or the wrong things. Forgetting should be principled, not accidental.
